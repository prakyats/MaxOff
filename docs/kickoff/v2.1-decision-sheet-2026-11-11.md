# MaxOff v2.1 "Reliable anywhere": decision sheet

Read-only pass over the `phase-v2` checkout (`bafd6f45`); nothing was run, written or committed.

**Five things in the brief do not match the code. They change the questions.**
- **Files live in Cloudflare R2, not Supabase Storage** (`src/core/storage/env.ts`, ADR-0003).
- **No submitted work is uploaded into MaxOff yet.** The only file kinds are logo, avatar, receipt and preview (`src/core/storage/limits.ts`). Submissions (8.1) and `core/drive` (8.3) are not built, so ADR-0010's Drive archive protects nothing today.
- **A production restore drill already happened on 30 Sep 2026**, on the first nightly backup, before real data. Unit 8 is the first timed drill on real data; the next is due by end of December.
- **Leave requests are already safe against two people acting at once.** They are never edited in place, and `leave_decide` refuses a second decision.
- **"v2.1" appears nowhere in `docs/`** in this checkout. Unit numbers below are taken from the brief.

## Part 1: for the Owner (tick one per question; my pick is first)

### Bad network

**1. Start day / End day with no network: should the phone save the tap and send it later?**
- **No. A tap counts when it reaches MaxOff; the phone says "No network, tap again." (Recommended)** The only time nobody can dispute is the one MaxOff saw itself.
- Yes, and the arrival time counts. People get marked Late for a bad signal; a tap near midnight can land on the wrong day.
- Yes, and the phone's time counts. Phone clocks can be changed, so times could be faked.

**2. Someone is Late only because their signal was down. What do you see?**
- **A line on their day, added by the phone: "Tried at 9:58, no network." Late stays until you correct it. (Recommended)** You get the fact and you decide.
- Nothing extra; they tell you themselves.
- Late is removed automatically. MaxOff would be deciding attendance instead of you.

**3. Someone taps Create, the signal drops, they tap again. What must never be made twice?**
- **Everything created often: tasks, task requests, projects, deliverables, clients, contacts, expense claims, comments. (Recommended)** Any duplicate costs clean-up time.
- Only tasks, projects, claims and comments. About a day less work; the rest can still double.

### Two people editing the same thing

**4. Two people change the same task, project, deliverable or client. What happens to the second save?**
- **Stopped only if both changed the same detail (say, the due date). They see who changed it, keep their typing, and pick "Use theirs" or "Save mine". (Recommended)** Nothing is lost silently, and there are no false alarms.
- Stopped whenever the record changed at all. Simpler, but interrupts people more.
- As today: the last save silently wins.

### The 10:00 rush test

**5. How many people starting their day in the same minute do we test?**
- **40, about double today's team. (Recommended)** Shows room to grow.
- 20. Proves today only.
- 100. Mostly tests the small test database, not MaxOff.

**6. What is a pass?**
- **Every Start day goes through, none doubled, and 19 of 20 people wait under 3 seconds. (Recommended)** Strict on correctness, fair to a small test database.
- The same, under 2 seconds. May fail because of the test database alone.
- Correctness only, no time limit.

**7. Where does it run? (Never on the live app.)**
- **On the test copy of MaxOff, after 10 pm, with made-up people. (Recommended)** Real servers, nobody disturbed.
- A developer laptop only. Safe, but the speed numbers mean nothing.
- A separate rented test database. Cleanest numbers; costs money and setup.

### Uploaded files

Today MaxOff holds logos, profile photos and expense-receipt photos, in one place with no copy.

**8. How do we protect them?**
- **A nightly locked copy in a second Cloudflare storage box, opened only with your backup key. (Recommended)** About a day's work, free at this size.
- Build the Google Drive archive now. Weeks of work, and it is meant for submitted work, not receipts.
- Wait. A storage accident loses every receipt photo.

**9. How long are the copies kept?**
- **Always; size checked once a year. (Recommended)** Receipts are money records and the files are small.
- One year.
- 31 days, like the database backup. A receipt lost unnoticed is gone after that.

### Restore practice

**10. When is the next practice restore on your laptop?**
- **Now, with v2.1: last night's backup, timed, checking yesterday's records are there. Then every three months, with an automatic reminder. (Recommended)** The only one so far was before real data.
- Wait for December.

**11. Your backup key is the only way to open a backup. Where is the spare?**
- **A sealed copy (USB or printout) somewhere only you can reach. (Recommended)** If the laptop dies, the backups still open.
- A trusted second person holds one. Covers you being away; one more person can read everything.
- No spare. Laptop lost means every backup is unreadable.

### Unit 2: repeat-safe creates, and offline attendance

**What happens today**
- `useAction` (`src/core/ui/action/use-action.ts`) sends one request per tap. For forms marked `creates: true` it offers no Retry after a lost reply, only "it may or may not have been saved" (`network-error.ts`). The person checks by hand; a second submit makes a duplicate.
- Forms marked `creates`: new project, new client, add contact, expense claim, holiday, suggest task, task chat composer, task stage, add person, coordinator, and task create (`task-form-dialog.tsx:409`).
- One precedent exists: `comp_leave_grant(..., request_key uuid default null)` with `comp_leave_credits.request_key` and a partial unique index (`20260928063232_phase3b_review.sql:393-446`), keyed from a `useRef` in `comp-leave-owner-actions.tsx`.
- Already repeat-safe through unique constraints or state checks: leave (same dates refused), extra-work notes (`unique(member_id, work_date, kind)`), holidays, invites, and Start/End day.
- Attendance: `attendance_start_day()` and `attendance_end_day()` (latest in `20261022121645_w0_test_clock.sql`) take no time argument. They stamp `app.now()` on arrival and derive the IST day and `late_minutes` from it.
- So a queued Start sent at 10:20 would be Late by server time, and one queued at 23:58 and sent at 00:03 would start the wrong day. A queued End after the 05:00 cutoff is refused.
- Offline today: `OfflineBanner` disables every commit button (ARCHITECTURE §14.1). Nothing is queued, and `public/sw.js` has no background sync.
- A retried Start that did land returns `INVALID_STATE` "Your day has already started", and the revalidated screen shows the started day. That is acceptable as is.

**Design (question 3, recommended option)**
- One ledger table, not a column per table: `public.request_keys (member_id, request_key uuid, kind text, entity_id uuid, payload_hash text, created_at, primary key (member_id, request_key))`.
- RLS on with no policies and no API grants; only functions write it.
- Helper `app.request_claim(key, kind, payload_hash) returns uuid` inserts the row. On a unique conflict it returns the stored `entity_id`, or fails with `CONFLICT` if the hash differs, as `comp_leave_grant` does for a different amount. A concurrent duplicate blocks on the key until the first commits.
- Helper `app.request_done(key, entity_id)` records the result.
- Additive typed wrappers, each one transaction, calling the untouched existing function: `task_create_once`, `task_request_create_once`, `project_create_once`, `project_create_grouped_once`, `item_add_once`, `item_add_grouped_once`, `client_create_once`, `expense_claim_submit_once`. Each takes `request_key uuid` plus the same arguments.
- Why wrappers: no 150-line body is re-copied, nothing is dropped, and a rolled-back Worker still calls the old functions. The other accepted route is the precedent's drop and re-create with a defaulted parameter.
- Plain PostgREST inserts (`task_comments` in `tasks/data/tasks.ts:1032`, `client_contacts` and the Owner's `clients` insert in `clients/data/clients.ts`): add a nullable `request_key uuid` column and a partial unique index on `(created_by or author, request_key)`. The data layer treats a 23505 on that index as "already saved".
- Client: a `useRequestKey(resetKey)` hook in `src/core/ui/action/` gives one `crypto.randomUUID()` per opened form, renewed after success. `useAction` gets a `keyed` option so a keyed create offers Retry after a lost reply.

**Design (questions 1 and 2, recommended options)**
- No queue and no schema change.
- Start and End stay tappable while offline, as an exception to the offline disable. The tap sends nothing, shows "No network. Tap again when you're back", and stores `{istDate, triedAt}` in `localStorage`.
- When Start later lands and the day is Late, the existing late-reason sheet is pre-filled with "No network. First tried 9:58 (phone time)". It goes through the existing `attendance_late_reason(reason)`.
- The server time stays the record. The Owner corrects through `attendance_decide`, so invariant 7 holds.
- End day gets the message only. A wrong end time is the Owner's correction, as today.
- If the Owner picks a queue option in question 1, stop: it needs a client-time column beside `started_at`, an ADR, and changes to both transition functions.

### Unit 5: two people editing the same thing

**What happens today**
- `task_update_assignment(task_id, changes jsonb, warnings)`, `project_update(project_id, changes jsonb)` and `item_update(item_id, changes jsonb)` receive only the changed keys. `updateTask` in `tasks/actions/tasks.ts:90` sends "only what changed".
- `item_update` locks the row with `app.item_lock` and diffs each key (`20261008154249_client_work_owner_answers.sql:473`).
- So edits to different details already both land. Edits to the same detail are last-write-wins with no warning; both appear in `activity_log`.
- `custom_fields` is replaced as one blob, so two different custom fields do collide.
- Plain tables use `.update().eq("id")` only: `clients`, `client_contacts`, `client_brand`, `client_private` (`clients/data/clients.ts:196-363`), `org_settings` and `organizations` (`settings/data/settings.ts`).
- On those, `count === 0` currently means "not yours" or "forbidden", so a naive `updated_at` filter would be misreported as a permission error.
- All these tables have `updated_at` and the `set_updated_at` trigger.
- The `CONFLICT` code and message already exist (`src/core/errors/codes.ts`).
- Leave needs nothing. Settings are mostly Owner-only, so the risk is his own two devices.

**Design (question 4, recommended option): no table change**
- Workflow edits: additive wrappers `task_update_checked`, `project_update_checked` and `item_update_checked`, each taking `(id, changes, expected jsonb, ...)`.
- A wrapper takes the same row lock the target takes, then checks every key in `changes`. If the current value differs from both `expected[key]` and the new value, it fails with `CONFLICT`, naming the detail and, from the latest `activity_log` row, who and when. Otherwise it calls the existing function in the same transaction.
- A whole-row `updated_at` check is the wrong tool here: a task's row changes on every state move, so an Admin editing a description would be blocked by a Crew member tapping Start.
- `custom_fields` is compared per custom-field key inside the wrapper.
- Plain tables: the form sends the `updated_at` it loaded as the untouched string (never through `Date`; microseconds matter), and the data layer adds `.eq("updated_at", expected)`. On `count === 0` it re-reads the row: visible with a different `updated_at` means `CONFLICT`; otherwise the existing refusal stands.
- UI: on `CONFLICT` the form stays open with the typing kept, and shows the message with two neutral actions. "Use theirs" refreshes the base values. "Save mine" re-sends with the fresh expected values; it is a deliberate overwrite and is audited.
- One solid action per layer (ADR-0015); red is not used.

Add a back-gesture spec for the notice at 375 and 430, and extend `e2e/freshness.spec.ts`.

### Unit 6: the 10:00 IST load test

**What 10:00 looks like in code**
- Per person: the `(app)` layout's first reads (member row, own day, push status, `session_bundle`, `session_early`), the role's home, then `startDay` (`attendance/actions/attendance.ts`).
- `startDay` runs `assertPermission`, `attendance_start_day`, `getOwnHours`, optionally `attendance_late_reason`, `setHomeHint` and `revalidateScreens`, then the screen re-renders.
- Each start takes `pg_advisory_xact_lock('leave:<member>')`, which is per member, so starts by different members do not block each other.
- Existing coverage is single-member only: `supabase/tests/09_start_day_concurrency.test.sql`.
- `scripts/perf-probe.mjs` is read-only and already mints staging sessions with the service key.

**Design**
- New `scripts/load-10am.mjs`: Node, fetch only, no new dependency, reusing `scripts/lib/`. Run as a new `load-test` choice in `preview.yml`'s manual dispatch.
- A host guard copied from perf-probe refuses anything but staging or its previews.
- People: N staging-only members with fixed recognisable ids, added by a guarded script that refuses any project ref other than staging. They are deactivated after each run and reactivated before the next, so nothing is deleted and perf-probe's "first active member" never picks one.
- Sessions are minted before T0, slowly. GoTrue allows 30 sign-ins per 5 minutes per IP (`supabase/config.toml`), and the Cloudflare rule on `/login` also applies.
- At T0, spread over 60 seconds, each person does: GET home through the Worker with their cookies; `attendance_start_day` through PostgREST with their own JWT; GET home again.
- Beside that, 5 real Playwright phone contexts tap the real button, to time tap-to-done through the server action. Scripting 40 server-action POSTs by action id is too brittle.
- Report: successes, 5xx count, and p50, p95 and max per step.
- Correctness: exactly one `attendance_days` row and one `started` event per member; no deadlock or `40001` in the results.
- Pass (question 6): zero failures, zero duplicates, tap-to-done p95 of 3 seconds or less. The first run is recorded as the baseline in RELEASE-NOTES.
- A member can start only once per IST day and history is never rewritten, so there is one run per night per member set.
- Run Monday to Saturday after 22:00 IST, never during a speed-gate "window", and announce it on the release issue.
- A local run against Docker comes first, for correctness only.

### Unit 7: backup of uploaded files

**What happens today**
- `backup.yml` backs up Postgres only. The dump does contain the `files` rows (`storage_key`, `sha256`, `size_bytes`), so a file manifest already exists inside it.
- Objects sit in one private R2 bucket. `storage_cleanup` (`src/core/storage/cleanup.ts`) deletes objects of archived rows after 30 days, pending ones after 24 hours and unreferenced ones after 7 days.
- A wrong deletion, a bad cleanup bug or a lost bucket is unrecoverable.
- ARCHITECTURE §17's last line, "R2 object versioning or retention (6.x)", is not implemented.

**Design (questions 8 and 9, recommended options): no database migration**
- A new job in `backup.yml` after the dump runs `scripts/backup/files-sync.sh`.
- It lists the files bucket and the backup destination. For each source key missing in the destination it streams GET, then `age -r <Owner's key>`, then PUT as `files/<bucket>/<storage_key>.age`.
- It is add-only: it never deletes or overwrites in the destination. Storage keys are unique and immutable (`files.storage_key unique`, protected columns).
- It reuses the existing failure-issue step.
- `scripts/backup/lib.sh`'s `s3_list` needs continuation-token paging; today it reads one page of at most 1000 keys.
- New `scripts/backup/restore-files.sh` decrypts into a fresh bucket or a local folder. It puts only missing keys and never overwrites a live object.
- `ops-check.yml` should also check that the files step ran.

**Owner setup steps (record in README "Backups" and `docs/runbooks/secrets.md`)**
- Create a new bucket, for example `maxoff-files-backup-production`, with no lifecycle rule.
- Do not reuse `maxoff-backups-production` as it stands. README step 5 sets its 31-day delete rule with an empty prefix, which would silently delete every file copy after a month. The alternative is to narrow that rule to `postgres/`.
- Create a read-only token on the files bucket and a write token on the new bucket, both in the `backup` environment.

**Tests**
- Extend `scripts/backup/drill.sh` on MinIO: upload three files, sync, delete one source object, restore, and compare sha256 with the `files` rows. A second sync must copy nothing. A listing of more than 1000 keys must page.
- No pgTAP and no Playwright; there is no schema or screen change.
- Add a manifest line for file count and bytes.

**Risks**
- Receipts are money evidence (invariant 2), so every object is encrypted and the new token is scoped to one bucket.
- R2's free 10 GB is per account, shared with the live files and the database dumps. Add it to the monthly usage check.
- `sha256` may be null on older rows; fall back to size.
- When submissions (8.1) arrive with 100 MB videos, revisit: ADR-0010's Drive archive takes over for originals.

**Size:** S to M (about 1 day, plus the Owner's bucket and token steps).

### Unit 8: the restore drill

**What happens today**
- `docs/runbooks/backup-restore.md` has the full path: `fetch.sh` and `restore.sh`, with verification against the manifest of row counts, migrations, RLS refusals and the pg_cron warning.
- The 30 Sep row records the Windows quirks on the Owner's laptop: `MSYS2_ARG_CONV_EXCL`, the `C:/` mount, and carriage returns in `python3` output.
- Gap audit 1: the timed drill on a recent backup with a "known recent record" check (6b.20 a) was skipped.
- The reminder is one line inside the monthly usage issue.
- Only the Owner can run it; the age private key never reaches CI or an agent.

**Design**
- 8a, now, with no dependency: `scripts/backup/drill-production.sh`, one command for Git Bash on Windows with the three quirks built in.
  - Secrets are prompted with `read -rs`, never placed on a command line.
  - It restores into a throwaway local `supabase/postgres` container with `AUTH_MODE=schema`.
  - It times download, decrypt, restore and verify.
  - It prints a ready-made "Drills done" row.
  - It removes the container and the decrypted folder at the end.
- Known-recent-record check: `dump.sh` adds `newest_activity_at` (max of `activity_log.at`) and the count of `attendance_days` for the previous IST date to the manifest. `restore.sh` verifies both, and fails if the newest activity is more than 26 hours older than the backup instant.
- Expected outcome is unchanged: "restore verified, with 1 warning(s)", the pg_cron warning.
- 8b, after Unit 7: the drill also decrypts three random file copies and compares sha256.
- Cadence: a scheduled job in `backup.yml` on the 1st of January, April, July and October opens a "Restore drill due" issue linking the runbook. The SLO runbook already proposes quarterly.
- Question 11 (spare key) is a runbook and `secrets.md` entry stating where the spare is. The key itself is never recorded.

**Files**
- `scripts/backup/drill-production.sh`, `dump.sh`, `restore.sh`, `drill.sh`
- `.github/workflows/backup.yml`
- `docs/runbooks/backup-restore.md`, `slo.md`, `secrets.md`, `incident.md`
- ARCHITECTURE §17

**Tests**
- `drill.sh both` stays green locally with the new manifest fields.
- A deliberately stale manifest makes `restore.sh` fail.
- No pgTAP and no Playwright.

**Risks**
- Never point the drill at a live project. The script should refuse any target host that is not `127.0.0.1`.
- An older backup without the new manifest fields must verify with a notice, not a failure.
- The drill needs Docker Desktop running on the laptop.

**Size:** S for the build; about 45 minutes of the Owner's time per drill.
